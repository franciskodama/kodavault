## Elliott Wave: Rules and Guidelines Summary

This summary outlines the structural rules and behavioral guidelines for Elliott Wave patterns as detailed in the technical documentation.

---

## 1. Motive Waves

Motive waves move in the direction of the one larger degree trend.

## Impulse Waves

- **Structure**: Always subdivides into five waves.
- **Subdivisions**: Waves 1, 3, and 5 are impulses; Wave 3 must be an impulse.
- **Core Rules**:
  - **Wave 2**: Never moves beyond the start of Wave 1.
  - **Wave 3**: Must move beyond the end of Wave 1 and is **never** the shortest wave.
  - **Wave 4**: Never moves into the price territory of Wave 1.
  - **Extensions**: Only one of the three motive subwaves (1, 3, or 5) is typically extended (stretched).
- **Guidelines**:
  - **Alternation**: Wave 4 is usually a different corrective pattern than Wave 2.
  - **Slope**: Wave 3 typically has the steepest slope.
  - **Channeling**: Wave 5 often ends near a line parallel to the 2-4 trendline, drawn from the peak of Wave 3.

## Diagonal Waves

Diagonals are motive patterns that occur at specific trend positions where an impulse would normally be.

- **Ending Diagonals**: Occur in Wave 5 or Wave C positions; all subwaves (1-5) are zigzags.
- **Leading Diagonals**: Occur in Wave 1 or Wave A positions.
- **Contracting vs. Expanding**: In contracting varieties, subwaves get smaller; in expanding varieties, they get larger.
- **Overlap**: Unlike impulses, Wave 4 in a diagonal usually ends within the price territory of Wave 1.

---

## 2. Corrective Waves

Corrective waves move against the trend of one larger degree.

## Zigzags (5-3-5)

- **Structure**: A three-wave pattern labeled A-B-C.
- **Rules**: Wave B never moves beyond the start of Wave A.
- **Guidelines**: Wave C almost always ends beyond the end of Wave A and is often equal in length to Wave A.

## Flats (3-3-5)

- **Structure**: A three-wave pattern where subwave A is never a triangle.
- **Rule**: Wave B must retrace at least **90%** of Wave A.
- **Variations**:
  - **Expanded Flat**: Wave B > 105% of Wave A, and Wave C ends beyond the end of Wave A.
  - **Running Flat**: Wave B > 100% of Wave A, but Wave C fails to exceed the end of Wave A.

## Triangles (3-3-3-3-3)

- **Structure**: Five waves (A-B-C-D-E) that are mostly zigzags.
- **Types**:
  - **Contracting**: Converging trendlines; subwaves stay within the previous wave's range.
  - **Barrier**: Same as contracting, but the B-D line is horizontal.
  - **Expanding**: Diverging trendlines; subwaves move beyond the previous same-directional wave.

---

## 3. Practical Application & Deductive Reasoning

- **The "Preferred Count"**: The interpretation that satisfies the highest number of rules and guidelines.
- **The "Alternate Count"**: Essential for risk management; it is the second-best interpretation kept in reserve if the market violates the preferred count.
- **Deductive Reasoning**: Use an "elimination process" to remove impossible patterns. As Sherlock Holmes stated: "When you have eliminated the impossible, whatever remains... must be the truth".
- **Objective Analysis**: Avoid "reading into" a chart what you want to see; if the market violates a rule, the count is objectively wrong and must be changed.
